# 02 Graphics approach: code-generated toon campus vs 3D modelling

How to draw the whole UW campus in an Untitled Goose Game inspired style in the browser: options compared, the recommended hybrid, a tested three.js r187 TSL recipe, palettes, OSM-to-style rules and a performance budget.

Read with [03-untitled-goose-game-deep-dive.md](03-untitled-goose-game-deep-dive.md) (what the look is) and [01-data-sourcing.md](01-data-sourcing.md) (where the geometry comes from). Code: [snippets/toon-recipe.mjs](snippets/toon-recipe.mjs).

## 1. Target look in one paragraph

Untextured, flat-coloured, low-contrast 3D; a two-tone lit and shadow split; no ink outlines; very faint soft shadows; a whiff of ambient occlusion in creases; muted pastels; rounded, chunky shapes; a high, narrow-FOV camera that makes the world read like a diorama; and the white goose with an orange beak as the highest-contrast thing on screen ([research/goose-game-research.md](research/goose-game-research.md) section 2, which cites Frieze, gameartstyles.com and Katherine Cross). Measured on the six official Steam screenshots in [research/raw/goose/](research/raw/goose/): mean HSV saturation 0.20 to 0.33 and mean value 0.49 to 0.61. Those two numbers become automated style tests (section 7).

Not the look: slop-newyork's photoreal-ish facades, PBR materials, bloom, or photo textures.

## 2. Options

| Option | What it means | World accuracy | Art quality | Effort for 2.3 x 1.8 km | Reproducible | Licence risk | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Fully procedural from data | Every building, path, tree and prop generated by code from OSM, City and lidar data; TSL shaders give the style | High (measured by IoU, RMSE) | Medium: risks looking like a generic extrusion | Low marginal cost: one pipeline covers all ~500 buildings | Yes, rebuilds from source | Low (data licences only) | Base layer |
| B. Hand-modelled in Blender | Artists model campus buildings from photos | Medium, drifts | High if skilled | Very high: days per landmark, months for campus | No | Low | Only for a few landmarks and props |
| C. Photogrammetry or 3D tiles | Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles or own drone scans | Highest | Wrong style (photoreal), heavy | Low to import, high to stylise | Partly | High: Google's tiles are under the Maps Platform terms, not open data (terms **not re-read for this doc**); drone flights near campus need permission | Reject |
| D. AI-generated 3D (text or image to 3D) | Generate props or buildings with a model | Low for buildings | Inconsistent style, messy topology | Low per asset, high cleanup | No | Copyright of AI outputs unclear; the course guide warns about "large blocks of generated content, especially images" ([research/course-constraints.md](research/course-constraints.md)) | Avoid, or disclose and use only for throwaway props |
| E. Hybrid (recommended) | A for the whole world; procedural "kit" rules per building class; hand-made low-poly hero props (bench, bin, bike, hard hat, WatCard, coffee cup) and 3 to 5 landmark overrides (DC, MC, SLC, Dana Porter, the Arts quad) | High | High where the player looks | Medium | Yes (overrides are versioned assets keyed by City `BLDG_ABBREV`) | Low | Recommended |

Why E: priority 1 is an accurate, reproducible world, which only A delivers at campus scale, and UGG itself got its look from clean shapes, a locked palette and animation, not textures, which code can produce. House House also bent real geography for play ([research/goose-game-research.md](research/goose-game-research.md) section 4.3, GDC 2021), so a hand-tuned override layer on top of data is faithful to the reference, not a compromise.

## 3. Procedural building kit (from footprint to toon building)

Input per building, from the pipeline: footprint polygon (City, conflated with OSM), parts with heights (lidar p90 or levels x 3.62 m), `roof:shape`, `building:colour`, `roof:colour`, `building:material`, City `BLDG_ABBREV` and `BUILDING_USE`.

1. **Simplify and regularise**: Douglas-Peucker at 0.3 m, then snap near-right angles to 90 degrees (helps the chunky look and cuts triangles). Keep the original for IoU tests.
2. **Chamfer vertical corners** by 0.15 to 0.3 m (2 segments) so silhouettes look rounded like UGG's bevelled props.
3. **Plinth**: the bottom 0.6 m extruded 0.1 m outward in a darker shade of the wall colour.
4. **Window bands as shader, not geometry**, by default: world-space storey bands in TSL (snippet section 5); geometric window insets only on landmark overrides.
5. **Parapet lip** on flat roofs: 0.4 m high, 0.25 m thick, roof colour; on gabled or hipped roofs (`roof:shape`), a simple straight-skeleton roof (OSM coverage is low: 47 of 341 campus ways have `roof:shape`).
6. **Roof plant**: a few boxes on large flat roofs, seeded by building id, so towers do not look like blank blocks from the high camera.
7. **Baked vertex colours**: AO from a cheap hemisphere ray test in the pipeline (or simple rules: darken inside corners, under overhangs and the bottom metre), plus a vertical gradient. Zero runtime cost and no flicker, which beats screen-space AO on iGPUs.
8. **Ground contact**: flatten terrain under each footprint to its median ground elevation; add a 1 m "skirt" below grade to hide seams on slopes.
9. **Merge per chunk** (256 m tiles) into one geometry per material class to keep draw calls low; keep a per-building id attribute for picking and highlighting.

Other world elements:

- **Paths**: ribbons along OSM ways with widths by type (doc 01, section 3.3), raised 2 cm, with rounded caps; materials by `surface` (asphalt grey, concrete pale, paving_stones warm). Kerbs as a 10 cm step on Ring Road.
- **Grass**: flat colour ground plus instanced tufts only near the camera (UGG uses small geometry tufts); a 2-tone noise variation in the ground shader.
- **Trees**: 3 to 4 low-poly archetypes (round deciduous blob, cone conifer, multi-blob maple, shrub), instanced, scaled by lidar canopy height, tinted by species group.
- **Water**: flat plane per lake at its level, a lighter edge band from distance-to-shore baked into a vertex attribute, gentle normal wobble in TSL; no reflections.
- **ION train and buses**: simple rounded capsules on the real rail line and GTFS routes.

## 5. three.js r187 recipe (WebGPU with WebGL2 fallback)

Versions checked on npm on Nov 2, 2026: `three` 0.187.0 (MIT, published Oct 30, 2026). Imports come from `three/webgpu` and `three/tsl`. The snippet [snippets/toon-recipe.mjs](snippets/toon-recipe.mjs) was smoke-tested in Node.js (it builds the node graphs and materials without a GPU); rendering is untested.

```js
import * as THREE from 'three/webgpu';
import { color, uniform, float, mix, smoothstep, step, fract, fwidth, abs,
         positionWorld, normalWorld, hash, vertexColor, fog, rangeFogFactor,
         pass, mrt, output, normalView } from 'three/tsl';
import { ao } from 'three/addons/tsl/display/GTAONode.js';

const renderer = new THREE.WebGPURenderer({ antialias: true /*, forceWebGL: true */ });
await renderer.init();                       // picks WebGPU, falls back to WebGL2
renderer.shadowMap.enabled = true;
renderer.shadowMap.type = THREE.PCFShadowMap; // PCFSoftShadowMap is removed in WebGPURenderer (warns and falls back)
```

Facade material with storey bands, no textures:

```js
function makeFacadeMaterial({ wall, band, storey = 3.6, seed = 0 }) {
  const m = new THREE.MeshToonNodeMaterial();      // two-step toon ramp by default; set m.gradientMap for custom steps
  const s = fract(positionWorld.y.div(uniform(storey)));
  const aa = fwidth(s).mul(1.5);                   // anti-alias the band edges so they do not shimmer
  const inBand = smoothstep(float(0.35).sub(aa), float(0.35).add(aa), s)
    .mul(float(1).sub(smoothstep(float(0.75).sub(aa), float(0.75).add(aa), s)));
  const isWall = float(1).sub(step(0.7, abs(normalWorld.y)));
  const jitter = hash(float(seed)).sub(0.5).mul(0.06);
  m.colorNode = mix(uniform(new THREE.Color(wall)), uniform(new THREE.Color(band)), inBand.mul(isWall))
    .add(jitter).mul(vertexColor());               // baked AO arrives as vertex colour
  m.vertexColors = true;
  return m;
}
```

Light, shadows, fog:

```js
scene.add(new THREE.HemisphereLight(0xfff6e5, 0x9fb59a, 2.2));   // strong, warm sky; sage ground
const sun = new THREE.DirectionalLight(0xffffff, 1.6);
sun.castShadow = true;
sun.shadow.intensity = 0.25;   // real LightShadow property: faint UGG-style shadows
sun.shadow.radius = 4;
scene.add(sun);
scene.fogNode = fog(color(0xdfe6d8), rangeFogFactor(250, 1400)); // haze in sky colour
```

Post (optional; baked AO is the default):

```js
const pipeline = new THREE.RenderPipeline(renderer);  // was PostProcessing before r183 (now deprecated alias)
const scenePass = pass(scene, camera);
scenePass.setMRT(mrt({ output, normal: normalView }));
const aoPass = ao(scenePass.getTextureNode('depth'), scenePass.getTextureNode('normal'), camera);
aoPass.resolutionScale = 0.5;
pipeline.outputNode = scenePass.getTextureNode('output').mul(mix(float(1), aoPass.getTextureNode().r, 0.35));
// in the frame loop: pipeline.render();
```

Other building blocks in r187, all confirmed present in the npm package on Nov 2, 2026: `toonOutlinePass(scene, camera, color, thickness, alpha)` in `three/tsl` (inverted-hull outline for toon materials; UGG has no outlines, so use only a same-hue, low-alpha edge for readability if playtests demand it), `SobelOperatorNode` and `OutlineNode` in `three/addons/tsl/display/`, `CSMShadowNode` (`three/addons/csm/CSMShadowNode.js`) for cascaded shadows over a 2 km world, `TileShadowNode`, `BatchedMesh` and `InstancedMesh`, `SkyMesh`.

Shadows over 2 km: one shadow map cannot cover the campus at useful resolution. Either cascaded shadows (`CSMShadowNode`, 3 cascades out to about 400 m) or a single shadow camera that follows the goose and covers about 150 m, with baked AO doing the work further out. The second is cheaper and fits the high, close UGG camera.

### WebGL2 fallback notes

- `WebGPURenderer` falls back to WebGL2 automatically; `forceWebGL: true` forces the WebGL2 backend for testing. Run every benchmark on both backends.
- Compute shaders (`compute`, `instancedArray`, `storage`) only run on WebGPU. Keep crowd simulation on the CPU (or in a worker) so both backends behave the same; use GPU compute only for optional effects.
- WebGPU availability (secondary sources, **verify at build time**; see https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status and https://caniuse.com/webgpu): Chrome and Edge 113+ on Windows, macOS and ChromeOS; Chrome 121+ on Android; Chrome on Linux from 144 on recent Intel GPUs; Safari 26; Firefox 141+ on Windows only. Lab machines and some teammates' Linux laptops may land on WebGL2, so the fallback is not optional.

## 6. Rules: OSM and City tags to style class

Evaluated in order; first match wins. Keys are City `BLDG_ABBREV` where it exists, else OSM tags.

| Rule | Style class | Wall | Roof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landmark override file has `BLDG_ABBREV` | as authored | | |
| `building:material` in concrete, `building:colour` greyish, or abbrev in MC, DC, HH, DP, ML, PAS, NH, B1, B2 | Brutalist concrete | `#c9c3b6` | `#a9a396` flat with parapet |
| `building:material` brick, `building=dormitory` or `residential`, abbrev in V1, REV, MKV, UWP, CLV | Brick | `#b77a63` | `#8e8e8a` |
| `building:material` glass or abbrev in E5, E6, E7, QNC, EIT, M3, EV3, STC | Glass and steel | `#9fb3b8` with lighter bands | `#a9a396` |
| `building=university` or `college`, no material | Limestone and cream | `#ddd3bd` | `#a9a396` |
| `building:colour` present | Nearest palette swatch to the tag colour (CIEDE2000) | | |
| `building=roof` (canopies) | Canopy, open sides | `#cfcabd` | |
| `building=shed`, `service`, `garage` | Utility | `#bdb7a8` | |
| Otherwise | Default cream | `#ddd3bd` | |

The abbreviation lists are my starting guesses from campus knowledge; **confirm each against the 2026 orthophoto** and fix in the override file. OSM tag coverage on campus is thin (`building:material` 64 of 341 ways, `building:colour` 42), so the abbrev table and photo sampling will carry most of it. A palette sampler in the spirit of aalghooneh's `tools/commons-palette.py` (slop-toronto) can read the City orthophoto roof colours per footprint and snap them to the palette.

## 7. Performance budget (targets, to measure in the benchmark harness)

Reference devices: an Intel Iris Xe or similar iGPU laptop (the floor), an Apple M-series laptop, and one discrete GPU.

| Item | Budget |
|---|---|
| Frame time | p95 under 16.7 ms on the iGPU at 1080p with render scale 0.75 to 1 |
| Draw calls | Under 300 per frame (merge per chunk and material, instance props and trees) |
| Triangles in view | Under 1.5 M |
| Visible chunks | About 9 to 16 chunks of 256 m at full detail, distant chunks as merged low-LOD blocks |
| Shadow | 1 directional map, 2048 px, following the goose (about 150 m) |
| NPCs | 30 smart NPCs full FSM, 300 to 500 ambient walkers instanced (see [05-simulation-and-npc-design.md](05-simulation-and-npc-design.md)) |
| Physics | Rapier step under 2 ms with 200 awake bodies |
| First load | Under 10 MB compressed before first frame; stream the rest |
| GPU memory | Under 500 MB |

Style tests (CI, headless Chrome screenshots on fixed camera paths):

- Mean HSV saturation of the frame between 0.18 and 0.35, mean value between 0.45 and 0.65 (UGG screenshots measure 0.20 to 0.33 and 0.49 to 0.61).
- No pixel colours far outside the palette except the goose and UI (histogram check).
- SSIM against the last approved screenshot above a threshold, to catch shader regressions that agents introduce.

## 8. Camera (graphics side)

- Perspective, narrow FOV (20 to 30 degrees), pitched about 45 to 55 degrees, pulled far back: diorama feel, minimal vertical convergence (estimates from screenshots in the earlier memo).
- Free 360 orbit is not UGG, but a whole-campus open world needs it. Compromise: snap yaw to 8 directions with smooth transitions, or allow free yaw with a strong default facing per area.
- Occlusion with 3 to 12 storey buildings is the main unsolved camera problem: raycast camera to goose with three-mesh-bvh and dither-fade (screen-space Bayer pattern) any building chunk in between, plus a silhouette of the goose when hidden. Prototype this in week 1; it can sink the look.

## 9. Open questions

- Free camera yaw vs UGG-style fixed angles in an open world (playtest both).
- Whether a subtle same-hue outline improves readability at campus distances (UGG has none).
- Seasonal palettes (autumn maples, winter snow) as a cheap wow feature; the 2026 spring orthophoto and archive imagery can drive colours.
