# Waste, recycling and compost bins

## The official UW standard
Source: Plant Operations "Shift: Zero" Receptacle Standard, https://uwaterloo.ca/plant-operations/services/environmental-services/shift-zero-waste-management/shift-zero-waste-standards/receptacle-standard
- **All campus receptacles are Clean River multistream units.**
- **Outdoor model: Clean River XS35** (`private/web/008_xs35-3.jpg`, a dimensioned product render):
  - **XS35-3 (3-stream):** 62.00 W x 19.00 D x 41.75 H in (157.5 x 48.3 x 106 cm). Corner posts rise to **47.75 in (121 cm)**.
  - **XS35-4 (4-stream):** 82.00 x 19.00 x 41.75 in (208 x 48 x 106 cm).
  - Bags are 34-37 in W x 42 in H.
  - **Outdoor stream order, left to right: Garbage, Recycling, Paper.** Indoor order is Garbage, Organics, Recycling, Paper.
  - **Appearance (from the render):**
    - **All-black** frame and body, with square corner posts that have small caps and stand proud of the top.
    - Each bay has a horizontal slot opening at the top front.
    - Each bay has a front label: Garbage on black, Recycling on **blue**, Paper on **grey**. Each label has a small header strip with the stream name and a pictogram panel below.
    - It has no backboard; the kickplate sign is 13.09 x 19.68 in.
- **Indoor and high-traffic models**, which may appear at building entrances and patios:
  - **TIM51** (23.75 x 23.25 x 49 in) and **TIM72** (47.5 x 18 x 49 in): black bodies with coloured lids (blue for recycling, grey for paper, black for garbage) and a backboard sign.
  - **XFT50** (71 or 94 x 22 x 50 in, 3 or 4 streams): an arched backboard sign.
  - **X50F-PZA** is the cardboard unit.
- Bags: black for garbage, clear for recycling and paper, certified compostable for organics.
- Signage follows the **"Shift: Zero"** brand. The `010_waste-sorting.jpg` photo shows black units with "SORT" and "JOIN" panels and an arched backboard.

## Other bins
- **Campus Compost** (student group) runs 2 compost tumblers ("compost cows").
- OSM (`private/data/osm_in_boundary.geojson`): 35 waste_basket and 8 recycling points. Coverage is sparse.
- The UW Plant Ops restricted dataset has **"sanitation containers", "recycling containers" and "cardboard dumpsters" layers**, which give real positions (see `../maps/NOTES.md`).
- Older and legacy outdoor bins also exist in photos. **Not verified:** I expect black steel-slat round bins and concrete-aggregate bins near older buildings.
- Large service **dumpsters / front-load bins** sit at loading docks.
