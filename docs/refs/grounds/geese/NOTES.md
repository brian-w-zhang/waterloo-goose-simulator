# Canada geese on campus

## Behaviour and season
- **Nesting season** is roughly **mid-March to May**; some sources say through June. The UW Goose Watch is posted for about an 8-week season each spring.
  - Geese are protected under the **Migratory Birds Convention Act**, so nests can't be disturbed.
  - The UW Daily Bulletin runs a goose-safety item each April (for example 2025-04-21: "How to avoid a goose attack").
- **Goose Watch map** (UW, since 2013): an official map of reported nests. The site gets 3,000-6,000 views per season. Sources: CBC 2016 and 2017.
- **Waddleloo:** a student-built map of goose sightings and nesting hotspots (CTV, Imprint).
- Earlier history: UW tried a **dog patrol** to deter geese (CBC).
- **Typical nest sites** from coverage and photos:
  - Building ledges and planters near entrances.
  - Parking lot islands ("Geese nesting in parking lot A").
  - Beside doors (a goose watching through the glass door of East Campus 5).
  - Lawn edges by Ring Road, ponds (the pond outside Engineering 2, Laurel Lake, Strauss Lake / Health Services pond), and Columbia Lake.
- **Behaviour to model:**
  - Pairs defend about a 5-10 m radius around the nest: hissing, neck extended low, wings half-open, charging.
  - Goslings appear late April to May.
  - Families walk in single file across Ring Road and stop traffic ("Canada Geese Move For No One").
  - Grazing flocks on large lawns (MC green, Village Green / fields, Columbia Lake fields) in summer and fall.
  - Big migratory flocks on Columbia Lake in fall.
  - Droppings on paths.
- **Signs:** I found no photos of physical "nesting goose" signs. Goose Watch is online. Some years have taped paper or cone warnings near nests; this is anecdotal and unverified.
- Other wildlife in photos: great blue heron and mallards (Laurel Lake), red-tailed hawk, ladybirds, gulls.
