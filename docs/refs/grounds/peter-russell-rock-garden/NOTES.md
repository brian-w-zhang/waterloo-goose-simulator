# Peter Russell Rock Garden

## Location and plan
**The official map is the key file:** `private/fp3654-peter-russel-rock-garden-map-final-final-ua.pdf`, rendered as `private/rockgarden_map-1.png` (plan) and `-2.png` (rock list). It is from the Earth Sciences Museum.
- **Bounded by:** MC to the north, QNC to the west, ESC (Earth Sciences & Chemistry) to the east, and Biology 1 (B1) to the south. SLC is off the NW corner; DC and CEIT/EIT are off the NE corner; South Campus Hall is off the SE.
  - OSM garden polygon centre: -80.54346, 43.47145.
- **Size from the map scale bar:** about **75 m E-W by 70 m N-S** of garden between the building edges.
- **Layout:**
  - Square paved walkways (grey unit pavers) frame the garden on all sides.
  - A **"Main Path"** runs diagonally from the NW corner (toward SLC) to the SE corner (toward SCH), and a second leg goes to the NE.
  - Grass areas are irregular blobs.
  - About **20 tree clumps**, deciduous and conifer.
  - A **heavy timber pergola** about 30 m long runs diagonally SW to NE through the middle. It has square posts and double beams and is covered in **wisteria**.
  - A round planter or bed of about 5 m diameter (or a large tree in a circle) is east-centre.
  - About **14 timber benches** sit around the edges.
- **85 numbered rocks** (map legend). The UW page says "more than 100"; EcoMap says "more than 70".
  - Rocks are large boulders, typically 1-3 m. Many are flat-topped and some have bronze plaques.
  - Examples:
    - 1-3: Lorrain Fm jasper conglomerate and quartzite (NW corner).
    - 41: sodalite syenite (blue).
    - 46: rose quartz.
    - 59: the 50th-anniversary gneiss (installed 2007).
    - 63: Tyndall Stone.
    - 65: nephrite jade.
    - 71: Acasta gneiss ("oldest rock in the world").
    - 75: North Mountain basalt columns.
    - 76: petrified wood.
    - 49 / 50: stromatolites.
    - 36: epidote garnet skarn.
  - The full list is on page 2 of the PDF.
- **History:**
  - Started as the "Geological Garden" by curator Peter Russell for UW's 25th anniversary. Sources say 1982 or 1986. It opened officially on 29 May 1987.
  - It began with 12 planned rocks.
  - It was renamed for Russell in 1999.
  - Rocks were placed by crane and bulldozer (`private/web/002-005`).

## Look (from photos and `../grounds-commons/NOTES.md`)
- Grey asphalt paths about 2 m wide with forks.
- Boulders set on mulch and grass.
- Low spreading junipers and yews, mature pines, and deciduous trees.
- Daffodils in spring.
- Black lamp posts with cap heads.
- Wisteria pergola over large cracked concrete slabs.
- Salvaged cylindrical stone column fragments.
- The Egg Fountain ("Break") is at the NE side (see `../public-art/`).
